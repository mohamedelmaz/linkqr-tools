How to Create a WhatsApp Link for Your Business (2026 Guide)

WhatsApp has become one of the most important communication channels for small businesses, freelancers, and customer support teams. A WhatsApp link lets customers start a conversation with a single tap across your website, emails, or checkout counter. In this guide, you will learn exactly what a WhatsApp link is, why every modern business should use one, how to build it step by step, and how to combine it with QR codes for offline use.

What is a WhatsApp link?

A WhatsApp link is a specially formatted URL that opens a WhatsApp chat with a specific phone number when clicked or tapped. The standard format uses the wa.me domain followed by the recipient's country code and phone number. If you add a text parameter, WhatsApp automatically fills the message field so the sender does not have to type anything. This simple mechanism removes friction from the first point of contact between a business and a potential customer.

For example, https://wa.me/15551234567?text=Hello opens a chat with a pre-filled message. On mobile, it launches the WhatsApp app. On desktop, it opens WhatsApp Web.

Why businesses need a WhatsApp link

Speed matters. Studies show that customers expect a response within minutes, not hours. A WhatsApp link gives buyers an instant way to ask about pricing, book appointments, request support, or confirm delivery details. Unlike email or contact forms, WhatsApp feels immediate and personal, which builds trust faster.

Small businesses especially benefit because WhatsApp does not require a separate app download for most users, and the interface is already familiar. You can place the same link on your website footer, Instagram bio, Google Business Profile, printed receipts, and email signatures.

Step-by-step creation

Creating a WhatsApp link takes only a few seconds. First, decide which phone number will receive the messages. Make sure the number is active on WhatsApp and can handle incoming inquiries during business hours. Next, write down the full international format: country code plus phone number, with no leading zeros, dashes, or spaces.

Adding a custom message

A pre-filled message sets the context before the customer even speaks. Instead of staring at a blank text box, the user sees something like "Hi, I found your café on Google and I'd like to know your opening hours." This small detail reduces the mental load on the sender and gives your team useful context immediately.

Keep custom messages short and friendly. Avoid long paragraphs that get truncated on small screens. If you use the link in different contexts, such as support versus sales, create separate links with different pre-filled messages.

QR code vs link: when to use which

Links work best in digital environments: websites, social media bios, email signatures, and chat apps. A user simply clicks or taps, and the conversation starts. QR codes, on the other hand, excel in physical spaces. Place them on table tents, window stickers, product packaging, event badges, and invoices. A customer with a smartphone can scan the code without typing anything.

The most effective strategy is to use both. Put the clickable link on every digital touchpoint and the QR code on every physical touchpoint. For a standalone QR image, use the WhatsApp QR Generator.

Best practices for business WhatsApp links

Test your link on both iPhone and Android before sharing. Some older devices handle the wa.me redirect differently. Also, verify that the receiving phone number is monitored by a real person during business hours; nothing damages trust faster than a WhatsApp link that leads to silence.

Use a dedicated business number if possible, rather than a personal phone. This keeps personal and professional conversations separate.
